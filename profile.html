Analog & Mixed-Signal Integrated Circuits

I am an undergraduate student in Electrical and Computer Engineering at Seoul National University. My research interests lie in energy-efficient analog and mixed-signal integrated circuits for communication and sensing.

I am currently studying high-speed interface circuits in the IC3 Lab, advised by Prof. Wooseok Choi. I am also designing an event-driven vision sensor pixel with the goal of low-power analog sensing.

Previously, I worked with Prof. Jungsuek Oh in the Wave Fusion Lab on equivalent-circuit modeling and experimental characterization of RF metasurface absorbers.

My research interests include high-speed interfaces, clock generation, data conversion, RF circuits, and low-power sensor interfaces.

Research interests

High-speed interfaces & clocking: High-speed wireline and optical interfaces, clock and frequency generation, and PLLs.

Data conversion & sensor interfaces: ADCs, event-driven sensing, and low-power sensor interfaces.

RF circuits: RF integrated circuits for communication and sensing.

I am also interested in exploring these circuit techniques for energy-constrained wearable systems.

Event-driven sensingJul 2026–present

Dynamic vision sensor pixel

Analog circuit design · 45-nm CMOS

Work: Developing a DVS pixel for low-power sensing, targeting 1 V operation and detection of 20% photocurrent changes.

Core idea: Explore reset control to suppress leakage-driven false events while balancing sensitivity, noise rejection, and power consumption.

Status: Circuit design and validation are in progress.

Clock generation · IC3 LabJan 2026–present

Hybrid phase-locked loops

B.S. thesis research · Advisor: Prof. Wooseok Choi

Work: Implementing and analyzing a published hybrid PLL architecture through behavioral modeling and Cadence circuit design.

Core idea: Combine a linear proportional path with digital integral and double-integral control to study bandwidth tracking and the trade-off between tuning range and frequency resolution.

Result: Behavioral simulations showed lock acquisition and more consistent loop response with bandwidth tracking.

View project details ↗
RF circuits · Amplifier designOct–Dec 2025

Wideband Amplifier Design

Team course project · Microwave Engineering

Work: Co-designed a wideband PCB amplifier in ADS.

Core idea: Tune double-stub matching networks and evaluate the PCB layout through EM-based circuit simulation.

Result: The final presentation reports an EM-simulated operating band of 1.6–6.3 GHz.

View project details ↗
RF modeling · Wave Fusion LabJan–Dec 2025

Wideband Metasurface Absorber

Research internship · Advisor: Prof. Jungsuek Oh

Work: Developed an equivalent-circuit-assisted design method for a screen-printed wideband absorber.

Core idea: Use an Equivalent Circuit Model (ECM) calibrated with full-wave simulations to explore resonances and impedance matching through fast circuit evaluations.

Result: A 3-mm prototype showed estimated absorptivity above 90% over 7.75–16.07 GHz at normal incidence.

View project details ↗
Digital systems · FPGA designDec 2024

FPGA Digital Clock

Team course project · Logic Design and Laboratory

Work: Co-developed a multifunction digital clock in Verilog on a Basys 3 FPGA.

Core idea: Integrate timing, setting, stopwatch, and alarm-game modules through mode control and a shared multiplexed display.

Result: Completed timekeeping, time/alarm setting, a centisecond stopwatch, and an LED game for dismissing the alarm.

Analog circuits · PCB designMar–Jun 2024

Analog Discomfort-Index Monitor

Team course project · Basic Circuit Theory and Laboratory

Work: Contributed custom footprints and PCB layout to a team analog discomfort-index system.

Core idea: Combine log/antilog multiplication, op-amp summation, and comparator thresholds for fan control.

Result: Intermediate LTspice results showed stepped control outputs; an initial PCB layout and 3D model were completed.
