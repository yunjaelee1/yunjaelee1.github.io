Hybrid phase-locked loops

Clock generation · IC3 LabJan 2026–present

B.S. thesis research · Advisor: Prof. Wooseok Choi

I am implementing and analyzing a highly digital PLL to understand how analog phase correction and digital frequency control work together. The project combines behavioral loop analysis with Cadence implementation of mixed-signal building blocks.

This project builds on the architecture of Yin et al. (IEEE JSSC, 2011). The diagram below illustrates its proportional and integral control paths; the Cadence schematics show my project implementation.

Separate fast phase correction from fine frequency control

A linear proportional path avoids TDC quantization in the fast control path. Digital integral control supplies fine frequency correction, while a slower double-integral path provides coarse tuning so the fine path can operate over a smaller range.

Keep the loop response consistent as frequency changes

Bandwidth tracking: Scale the proportional-path gain with reference frequency to keep the ratio of loop bandwidth to reference frequency approximately constant.

Quantization-error tracking: Scale the integral-path frequency step with output frequency to reduce phase-error fluctuations at lower operating frequencies while retaining tuning range at higher frequencies.

From control paths to circuit blocks +

Switched-RC filtering: A low-pass filter smooths the DSM-DAC output. Gating a clock with its delayed version creates a nearly fixed pulse width, making the switch duty cycle—and therefore the effective resistance and filter bandwidth—track clock frequency.

What the behavioral simulations showed

The loop acquired frequency and phase lock. With bandwidth tracking, settling behavior remained more consistent as reference frequency changed. Scaling the frequency step reduced phase-error fluctuations at lower output frequencies. These are qualitative behavioral-model observations.

What I learned: How phase detection, loop gain, oscillator tuning range, and DAC resolution interact—and how to translate an architectural idea into circuit-level building blocks.

Architecture reference: W. Yin, R. Inti, A. Elshazly, B. Young, and P. K. Hanumolu, “A 0.7-to-3.5 GHz 0.6-to-2.8 mW Highly Digital Phase-Locked Loop With Bandwidth Tracking,” IEEE Journal of Solid-State Circuits, vol. 46, no. 8, pp. 1870–1880, 2011. DOI: 10.1109/JSSC.2011.2157259.
